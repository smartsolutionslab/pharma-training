# 0036: Erasure is requested in Organisation and announced to every service

**Status:** Accepted (#115, slices 1 to 5). Relies on ADR 0002 (409 through the use case), 0003, 0004 (mail from a durable local message), 0011 (publishing grants derived from code), 0015 (where names live), 0027 (error codes) and 0034 (retention as a cut-off).

## Context

- A person may ask for their data to be erased (GDPR Art. 17). The owner decided on 2026-11-21 that this is in MVP 1, a staff action through a support role with no self-service; that the pharmacy's certificates keep the holder's name for the retention period (Art. 17(3)(b)); that an erasure is refused while the person is a pharmacy's last active manager; and that a confirmation mail goes out before the address is removed, with an erasure log that holds no personal data.
- Names and addresses live in Organisation (memberships, invitation recipients and senders), in copies in Compliance and Certification, and as reviewer names in Editorial (docs/domain/README.md, Integration Events). Organisation owns names, addresses and the last-manager rule.
- #115 is delivered in slices from `agent/115` (Q8). Slice 1 is Organisation's own part; Compliance, Editorial and Learning, Certification, and the Keycloak account follow.

## Decision

- **Support requests the erasure in Organisation.** `POST /organisation/erasures` with `{ personId }` answers 201 with `Location: /organisation/erasures/{id}`; `GET /organisation/erasures/{id}` reads the log entry back (404 `organisation.erasure-not-found`). Both require the policy `organisation.erase-persons`, which the realm role `support` holds; the local user `support` holds it, and `developer` does not, so an end-to-end run cannot erase people. The work is API only; a support screen is a follow-up (Q7).
- **The request is one commit, refused before anything changes.** `RequestErasure` loads every pharmacy where the person has a membership or one of their addresses is invited, and checks each (`Pharmacy.MayReleaseForErasure`) before changing any. While the person is the last active manager of one, it answers 409 `organisation.erasure-refused-last-manager` and saves, publishes and mails nothing. Otherwise it ends the person's active memberships (announced as `MembershipEndedV1`, as `EndMembership` does), revokes the open invitations to their addresses, logs the erasure with the requesting staff member and requests the confirmation mail as the local `ErasureConfirmationMailRequestedV1`. A lost race answers the existing 409 `organisation.concurrent-save` (no new code: the save touches several pharmacies and names none).
- **The confirmation mail goes first, then Organisation forgets.** `ErasureConfirmationMailRequestedV1Consumer` runs from the host's durable local queue (ADR 0004). It mails each of the person's addresses a German plain-text confirmation that names neither the person nor a pharmacy and promises nothing not yet true when it is sent: it says the erasure was requested, the memberships are ended and the invitations withdrawn, the name and address leave the membership records right after the mail and the rest of PharmaTraining in the following steps, certificates a pharmacy must keep stay until their retention period ends, and questions go to Support. An address the mail server refuses for good (a 550, 551 or 553 reply to RCPT TO with an enhanced code 5.1.x, a bad mailbox: `MailNotSentException.RecipientRefused`) is given up and logged by the erasure's id only, and the erasure goes on; the confirmation counts as `Mailed` when at least one address took it. A refusal that is the relay's or the account's (530, 5.7.x) or carries no enhanced code is retried like any failure, since a misconfigured relay answers every address so, and giving each up would erase the person without a confirmation. Then, in one commit, it sets name and address to NULL on every ended or revoked membership of the person or to their addresses (on another person's membership that held one of the addresses, only the address: that person's name is not the erased person's data), deletes their invitation recipients and sender rows, records the confirmation (`Mailed`, or `NoAddress` when Organisation held none, Q5) and Organisation's step, and publishes `PersonErasureRequestedV1(ErasureId, PersonId)`. "Pseudonymise" means the name becomes NULL and the PersonId stays as the pseudonym (Q3).
- **Idempotent by state.** A handled confirmation is never mailed again; a step already recorded stays as it was. A send that fails forgets nothing and rethrows, so Wolverine retries and dead-letters; an SMTP outage holds the erasure until the mail goes out (Q6). A membership invited or active again by the time the mail goes out is skipped and logged.
- **The log holds no personal data.** `organisation.erasures` holds the id, when and by whom it was requested, the confirmation (stored as text, an enum because the strict DDD rule forbids a `bool` in an aggregate) and when it was handled; `organisation.erasure_steps` holds one row per module that completed. Neither holds the erased person's id, name or address. The aggregate raises no domain events: the contract event announces the erasure.
- **Every other service erases on `PersonErasureRequestedV1`** in later slices, each consumer idempotent by state, reporting completion with an event of its own module that Organisation records as a step. Certificates still in a pharmacy's view under ADR 0034 are kept with the holder's name and deleted, row and PDF, once no covering pharmacy still keeps them under its current retention period (ADR 0039; the longest covering period wins), the same rule the erasure applies; a period lengthened after the deletion does not bring one back (the owner's decision of 2026-11-25, replacing Q1's fixed 5 years); a kept certificate stays publicly verifiable until then (Q2); while one is kept, Learning's and Assessment's data stays pseudonymous rather than anonymous (Q4, Art. 17(3)(b)).

## Consequences

- `organisation.memberships.email` is nullable (migration `AddErasures`); the filtered unique index on open memberships is unaffected. Code that reads an address reads open memberships only.
- A send that returned before a later send or the commit failed mails the address again on retry, the same gap ADR 0004 accepts for invitations. An invitation to the person's address created between the request and the mail stays open and is logged.
- **Compliance (slice 2).** `PersonErasureRequestedV1Consumer` writes the name-free tombstone `compliance.erased_persons` (also for a person Compliance never saw, so a late membership event cannot write the name), sets `display_name` to NULL on every member and membership change of the person (migration `AddErasedPersons`, which also makes the column nullable), cancels their open and in-progress assignments in every pharmacy as `MembershipEndedV1Consumer` does, purges their stored membership messages (`IPersonalMessagePurge`: dead letters and handled incoming envelopes of `MembershipStartedV1`/`MembershipChangedV1` whose body holds the PersonId; never the outbox) and publishes `ComplianceErasureCompletedV1`, which Organisation records as the step `compliance`. The membership consumers drop and log (without the name) any event of a tombstoned person. All three hold the person with a transaction-scoped advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock` on the person's id) before reading anything of theirs, so a membership event handled at the same time as the erasure either commits the name before the erasure reads it, or waits and finds the tombstone. The purge commits on its own connection before the consumer's commit; a redelivered erasure purges again.
- **Editorial and Learning (slice 3).** Editorial's `PersonErasureRequestedV1Consumer` holds the person with a transaction-scoped advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended('editorial.person:<id>', 0))`, which begins the unit of work's transaction), writes the name-free tombstone `editorial.erased_persons` (migration `AddErasedPersons`; also for a person Editorial never saw), and forgets the person's name through `IReviewerNames`: two `ExecuteUpdateAsync` statements set `course_version_questions.reviewed_by_name` and `course_version_returns.returned_by_name` to NULL where `reviewed_by` / `returned_by` is the person, keeping the PersonId, the approval's time and the return's reason. Each statement runs at once inside that transaction, so the tombstone, both updates and the completion commit together; outside a transaction each would commit by itself. Only after both updates does it publish `EditorialErasureCompletedV1` and commit. It is a data-protection update of historical rows, not a change of a course version: no version is loaded and no domain event is raised.
  - **Why Editorial keeps a tombstone.** The first cut kept none, reasoning that an erased person has no account to approve or return with. But their Keycloak account and tokens outlive the erasure until slice 6, and a next version drafted while the erasure runs copies the names it loaded, so a name could come back after the step was recorded. `ApproveQuestion` and `ReturnToDraft` therefore hold the acting person and record a tombstoned one under their id with a NULL name (a return keeps its reason), and `DraftNextVersion` holds every reviewer whose approval it copies (in id order, so two drafts cannot deadlock) and copies a question approved by a tombstoned one approved, without the name. Each either commits before the erasure (whose updates then forget the name) or after it (and finds the tombstone). The Domain takes a nullable `ReviewerName` on approve and return; `DraftNext` takes the set of erased reviewers whose names the copy leaves out.
  - **Learning.** Its tables hold the PersonId only, the pseudonym, so its consumer purges its stored `MembershipStartedV1`/`MembershipChangedV1` messages as Compliance does, then publishes `LearningErasureCompletedV1`. Organisation records the two completions as the steps `editorial` and `learning`.
  - **Residual: a membership message in flight.** The purge removes what Wolverine's storage holds when it runs. A `MembershipStartedV1` or `MembershipChangedV1` of the person still in flight then (in the inbox not yet handled, or scheduled for a retry) can later be handled or dead-lettered with the name in Learning's or Compliance's Wolverine storage. A handled envelope expires under Wolverine's retention of handled envelopes; a dead letter stays until someone purges it by hand or the erasure is delivered again (`PersonErasureRequestedV1` replayed), which purges again.
- **Certification (slice 4).** `PersonErasureRequestedV1Consumer` holds the person (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended('certification.person:<id>', 0))`), writes the name-free tombstone `certification.erased_persons` (migration `AddErasedPersons`; also for a person Certification never saw), and splits the person's certificates at Certification's clock (`TimeProvider`).
  - **Removed versus retained.** A certificate is retained while it is still in some pharmacy's view under ADR 0034: a membership of the holder covers its `IssuedAt` (start inclusive, end exclusive, ADR 0032; `Membership.Covers`, the rule the export applies) and that membership's pharmacy still keeps it, `Certificate.IsKeptByPharmaciesAt(now, period)` with the period the pharmacy chose (ADR 0039, 5 years when it chose none). With two covering memberships, the pharmacy that keeps it longer decides. The periods are read at the erasure: a period lengthened afterwards does not bring back a certificate the erasure removed. A retained certificate is kept unchanged, name and PDF included (decision 3): it stays in the pharmacy's export until that pharmacy's period runs out and keeps answering the public verification (Q2); its purge is Q1 / slice 5. Every other certificate (issued outside every membership of the person, or older than every covering pharmacy's period) is removed, so the verification answers it with the existing 404 `certification.certificate-not-verifiable`. The split uses the membership endings Certification holds at the erasure: a `MembershipEndedV1` it has not applied yet (still retrying, or arriving after the erasure event) leaves the membership open, so a certificate issued after that ending counts as covered and is retained until the purge (slice 5) finds no covering pharmacy keeps it. Organisation publishes the ending well before any erasure of the person, so this needs a retried ending. The holder's name is set to NULL on every membership of the person (migration `AllowErasedHolderNames`); the rows stay, since their periods choose the retained certificates for the export. The stored membership messages are purged as in Compliance, and `CertificationErasureCompletedV1` is published in the same commit, recorded by Organisation as the step `certification`.
  - **Blob-first deletion.** For each removed certificate the PDF is deleted first, by its derived name `certificates/{id}.pdf`, whether or not one was stored (a render in flight may write it), through `IBlobDeletes` (`BuildingBlocks/Storage`; a missing blob is success, any other failure is thrown); only then do the rows go, with the commit. A run that fails between the two leaves the rows, never a PDF without its row, and the retry deletes what is left.
  - **The render hold.** `CertificateDocumentRequestedV1Consumer` loads the certificate, then holds its holder as the erasure does, and, when the holder is tombstoned, drops the request only if the erasure removed the certificate (asked of the database after the hold); a retained certificate is still rendered, since it keeps its name and PDF (decision 3); a request whose certificate is gone is logged and dropped, not thrown. So a render either commits its PDF before the erasure reads the certificates (and the erasure deletes it), or waits and finds the tombstone, and no PDF is written back after the erasure deleted it. `MembershipStartedV1Consumer` and `TestPassedV1Consumer` hold the person too and drop a tombstoned one, logged without the name, before the holder's name is looked up, so no name is written back and no certificate is issued after the erasure.
  - **Transient residual: the dropped message's handled envelope.** The consumers of Compliance and Learning that drop a tombstoned person's membership message purge the stored copies holding the person's id (Certification's membership consumers only drop and log; their purge is #439), since the message may have been in flight past the erasure's purge (Learning now keeps a tombstone `learning.erased_persons` for this, held as in the other services). The envelope of the very message being dropped is marked handled by Wolverine only after the consumer returns, so it is not among what that purge finds: it holds the name until Wolverine's retention of handled envelopes removes it, or a later purge (a replayed erasure) does. No table and no dead letter keeps the name.
- **Certification, slice 5: the purge of retained certificates.** `PurgeErasedCertificates`, sent every interval by a `PeriodicCommandService` (`AddPeriodicCommand`, `BuildingBlocks/Messaging`; the first pass one interval after start), looks again at every tombstoned person who still holds a certificate (`IErasedPersonStore.HoldingCertificatesAsync`: one query, an `EXISTS` on `certification.certificates` per tombstone, in id order). The interval is `Certification:ErasedCertificatePurge:Interval`, once an hour by default (the first pass comes one interval after the host starts, so a daily default would never run on a service restarted more often than once a day), from one second to seven days; any other value stops the host at startup, naming the key. For each person it takes the same hold as the erasure and the membership, passed-test and render consumers, applies the erasure's rule again with the periods the pharmacies keep now (`ErasedCertificates`, shared with the erasure consumer): a certificate goes once no membership covering its issue belongs to a pharmacy that still keeps it, the longest covering period deciding and 5 years where a pharmacy chose none. The PDFs go first, then the rows with that person's commit, which also ends the hold; a failure stops the pass and the next one deletes what is left. A person whose deletion keeps failing (a blob that cannot be deleted) therefore holds up every person after them in id order until it is fixed; each pass logs the failure as an error. The purge reads a pharmacy's period without a lock, so a period lengthened in the same instant as a pass may lose to the shorter one it read; the window is a pass's few reads per person. A period lengthened after the deletion does not bring a certificate back. The purge publishes nothing: Organisation recorded the step `certification` at the erasure, and the verification answers a purged certificate with the existing 404 `certification.certificate-not-verifiable`.
- Until slice 6 lands, the person's Keycloak account remains; `GET` shows which steps are done.
